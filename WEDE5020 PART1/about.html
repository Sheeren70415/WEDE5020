About the NSPCA

The National Council of Societies for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (NSPCA), founded in 1955, is South Africa's oldest and largest animal welfare organisation, dedicated to preventing cruelty and promoting the welfare of all animals across the country. . Operating as a statutory body under the SPCA Act, the NSPCA coordinates a network of member SPCAs and enforces animal protection laws through inspections, investigations, and legal action where necessary, covering domestic, farm, wildlife, and research animals. . Its mission focuses on ending animal abuse, uplifting animal welfare, and fostering compassion, while its vision aims to create a society where all animals are treated with respect. . The organisation comprises specialised operational units, including Wildlife Protection, Farm Animal Protection, Animal Ethics, and Special Investigations, which ensure comprehensive coverage of animal welfare issues. . Over the years, the NSPCA has been involved in landmark cases, public education campaigns, and humanitarian efforts, such as disaster relief for affected animals and legal actions to uphold statutory protections, all supported through donations and public engagement rather than government funding.

Our Mission

The NSPCA’s mission is to prevent cruelty to animals and promote their welfare across South Africa. The National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (NSPCA) is dedicated to protecting animals from abuse, neglect, and exploitation while promoting responsible care and compassion. Their mission focuses on ensuring that animals live free from suffering, receive appropriate care, and benefit from legal protections and humane treatment. The organization actively engages in rescue operations, investigations, community education, and advocacy for stronger animal welfare laws. By raising awareness and educating the public on ethical treatment of animals, the NSPCA strives to create a society where all animals are valued and safeguarded, reflecting a commitment to both practical intervention and long-term cultural change in the treatment of animals.
